What is a compatible console?

A compatible console is a game machine made by someone other than the original manufacturer, designed so that the same software can run on it. In practice, that often means you can insert your own cartridges and play them much as you would on the original hardware.

Hardware of this kind has long been sold and used in public, in various forms — for example as computer peripherals or as compatible machines for older consoles. This is a general description. It is not a statement about whether any other company’s product is lawful.

INRESET’s Mark 16 is a compatible console for MEGA DRIVE* / GENESIS* cartridges you already own, and for games on a microSD card. It uses an FPGA to reproduce circuits close to those of the original console.

Is a compatible console illegal?

Making hardware that works in the same way is not, by itself, generally illegal. What matters is not copying other people’s copyrighted programs or data (such as game ROMs or BIOS) or distributing them without permission, and not using another company’s trademarks or logos improperly. The answer can also depend on the country, region, and the facts of each case. We cannot say it is “completely legal everywhere.”

Mark 16 is designed with these points in mind:

  • No game ROMs or BIOS are included in the console, and we do not distribute them.
  • The FPGA circuits are INRESET’s modifications and integration of publicly documented technology and open-source FPGA cores. The Pico software that handles the menu, SD card, controllers, and saves is written by INRESET (and also uses publicly available development software). The open-source projects we use are credited under Patents, trademarks, and copyright.
  • It is not an official SEGA product, and it is not affiliated with, approved, or endorsed by SEGA. Names such as “MEGA DRIVE” are used only to describe compatibility. We do not use SEGA’s logos.
  • Please use only cartridges and data that you legally own. INRESET does not support or help with pirated ROMs.

Patents, trademarks, and copyright

These words sound similar, but they protect different things. In simple terms, there are three kinds:

Patents

A patent protects an “invention,” such as a new mechanism or device. For example, a technical idea about a way to display a picture can be the subject of a patent.

In Japan, a patent generally lasts 20 years from the filing date of the application (Japan Patent Office, “Patent” FAQ: duration of a patent right; see also the JPO primer in Japanese). In limited cases, such as certain pharmaceuticals, the term can be extended.

How this relates to Mark 16: the original MEGA DRIVE* console was released in 1988. The original hardware patents from that period are generally considered to have expired. That said, later filings and differences between countries can exist, so we cannot say that “every patent has expired” or that there is “no issue anywhere.”

Trademarks

A trademark protects names, logos, and similar signs that identify whose product or service it is. You must not use another party’s name or logo in a way that makes a product look official when it is not.

How this relates to Mark 16: the console uses INRESET’s own name and logo. Other companies’ product names are used only to describe compatibility, and we do not use official logos. Mark 16 is not an official SEGA product and is not affiliated with, approved, or endorsed by SEGA. “MEGA DRIVE,” “GENESIS,” and related names are registered trademarks of SEGA CORPORATION.

Copyright

Copyright protects creative expression, such as programs, game data, BIOS, pictures, music, and writing. Just as you must not copy a book and give it away, you must not copy and distribute a game ROM without permission.

How this relates to Mark 16: no game ROMs or BIOS are included or distributed. The FPGA circuits and Pico software are INRESET’s own work or are based on open-source projects that are credited. Please use only cartridges and data that you legally own.

What is an FPGA?

An FPGA is a chip whose internal circuits can be rewritten. Unlike an ordinary chip whose insides are fixed at manufacture, you can later change how the circuits are connected.

An “emulator” on a PC or phone reproduces a console’s behavior by calculating it in software. An FPGA, by contrast, builds the circuits themselves inside the chip. Timing is therefore closer to the original hardware, and the delay from a button press to the picture on screen (latency) is easier to keep low.

Mark 16 uses an FPGA to reproduce the main MEGA DRIVE* circuits, including the CPUs, video, and sound.

How Mark 16 works

The roles break down into a simple flow:

Your cartridge, or a game on a microSD card, is first received by the Pico (WeAct RP2350B). The Pico handles the menu, SD card, USB controllers, and save data. A cartridge is copied into RAM and then run from there.

The game itself runs on the Tang Primer 25K FPGA board (Gowin GW5A-25). Circuits for the CPUs (68000 and Z80), video (VDP), and sound (YM2612 and SN76489) are built inside the FPGA. Picture and sound go to a TV or monitor through DV output.

The microSD root folder is /MARK16 (older Mark16 and MDTANG cards still work). The menu has “Boot cartridge”, “Game list”, “Save cartridge”, and “Settings”. Firmware updates are applied from Settings → Console update.

Which software works?

Most MEGA DRIVE* / GENESIS* software runs. Mark 16 supports domestic and overseas cartridges, and games on a microSD card (root folder /MARK16) or in the console’s built-in flash. The menu’s “Save cartridge” item can store an inserted cartridge to the card or to built-in flash.

Compatibility is still being tested, and we do not guarantee that every title will work. Titles checked on hardware are listed on the software compatibility list. Support for Master System* and other 8-bit systems is also planned.

Can I save?

Yes. On the development hardware, original in-game saves are supported, along with save states (storing your progress in slots and resuming later) and screenshots. Data is stored on a microSD card. Save states still have problems in some software.

When you start a game from a cartridge, the save is written to the microSD card, not back into the cartridge, because the cartridge is copied into RAM to run. You can also back up saves and save states with the Mark16Manager PC app (v0.2.3, Windows / Apple silicon and Intel Mac).

Can I take screenshots?

Yes. On the development hardware, screenshots are supported. However, games that switch colours or scrolling partway down the screen (such as raster scrolling effects) may not be captured correctly. These games are marked △ or × in the Screenshot column of the software compatibility list. This does not affect gameplay or save states.

What about controllers?

There are two USB ports for controllers, so two people can play at once. USB controllers are supported. Plug in a wired Xbox controller (XInput) to use it as an analog controller. On supported games such as After Burner II, the stick works like an XE-1AP analog pad; in regular games, it also works as the D-pad. The triggers are analog too: in supported games, RT (and right stick up) raises the throttle, and LT lowers it. Xbox Start (≡) is START, and the center Xbox button opens the console menu. Whether a controller will be included with the product is still under consideration.
